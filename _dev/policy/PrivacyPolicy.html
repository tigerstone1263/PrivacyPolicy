6. Automatic collection and your choices

The SDKs built into the app (software from the companies in section 5) automatically collect the identifiers below and the device and usage information in section 2.7 while you use the app, and send them to those companies. They work much like cookies on a website. Google AdMob and Meta use the information they collect to show and measure ads.

  • Advertising and analytics identifiers: the advertising ID and app set ID (Android), the identifier for vendor (iOS), identifiers Firebase creates for each app installation, and identifiers created by Amplitude, Microsoft Clarity and Meta. They are used for app analytics and advertising and are kept as described in section 7.
  • Device identifier for sign-in: the Android ID or identifier for vendor (section 2.7), used to keep one account from being used on two devices at once. Sign-in needs it, so it cannot be turned off; it is deleted when you delete your account.
  • Notification token: used by Firebase Cloud Messaging to deliver notifications.

How to refuse or limit collection:

  • Users with a subscription do not see banner or full-screen ads. Rewarded ads play only when you choose to watch one.
  • Where the law requires consent for personalized ads (for example in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom), Google's consent form appears when the app starts. You can change your choice at any time in My Page > Settings > GDPR Settings.
  • On Android, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your device settings. On iOS, the app does not ask for access to your device's advertising identifier (IDFA).
  • You can turn off notifications in the app's notification settings or in your device settings.
  • The app has no setting to turn off only the analytics tools (Google Analytics for Firebase, Amplitude and Microsoft Clarity). If you do not want them, you can stop the collection by deleting the app.

If you delete your advertising ID or refuse personalized ads, you can still use every feature of the app. Ads still appear, but they may not be based on your interests.

7. How long we keep information and how we destroy it

  • Account record, device registration and sign-in account: until you delete your account. If you were a guest and then sign in to an existing account on that device, the guest account and its record are deleted.
  • Hall of fame: your entry stays until higher scores replace it.
  • Weekly leagues: deleted 8 weeks after the league week ends.
  • Friend requests and friends lists in other users' records: until the other user accepts or declines your request, or until their app finds that your account no longer exists.

  • Analytics, session replay and advertising data: kept by each provider for the retention period set in our account or by its own policy (for example, Microsoft Clarity keeps playback data for 30 days).
  • Information on your device: until you delete the app or its data.

How we destroy information

  • When a retention period ends or the purpose of processing has been achieved, we delete the information without delay.
  • If a law requires us to keep certain information longer, we keep it separately from other information, only for that purpose and period.
  • Electronic files are deleted so that they cannot be restored. Printed records, if any, are shredded or burned.
